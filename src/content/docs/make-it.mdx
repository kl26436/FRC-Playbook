A finished design is a list of parts somebody has to make. This chapter turns that list into work for the shop: what each part is, which machine makes it, in what order, and how to tell it is right.

## What we decided

- **Every made part has a number.** The letters say which subsystem it belongs to: D drivebase, E elevator, W wrist, C claw, F funnel, CL climber, EL electronics, BU bumpers.
- **Every part has one page.** It shows what the part is, the steps to make it, what to check, and its drawing. [See every part](../parts/).
- **Flat parts are cut from files.** Each plate has a flat pattern drawn at full size for the router. Inside corners are drawn at 0.13 in radius so a 1/4 in end mill can cut them.
- **Two fasteners do most of the work:** #10-32 screws and 5/32 in rivets. That keeps the drill list short.

## How to read a part page

1. **The facts** at the top: how many to make, what from, and on which machine.
2. **The flat pattern**, for router parts, with every hole listed by size.
3. **How to make it:** numbered steps, in order.
4. **Check it:** what must be true before the part leaves the bench.
5. **Print this as a job sheet** gives one page for the machine, with a code to scan back to the drawing.

## How close is close enough?

A drawing gives a size. It does not always say how far off is still fine. Three rules from the drawings cover most parts:

- **Tube length:** the elevator drawing asks for ends cut square to within 0.010 in. The two uprights of one stage must match each other, which matters more than either being exact.
- **Rivet holes:** drill them through one part, clamp it to its mate, and drill through. The holes then line up whatever the tape measure said.
- **Bearing bores:** the bearing must press in and not rock. Test the cut in scrap, because a few thousandths decides it.

## Your turn

For a new robot:

- [ ] Give every made part a number and one line saying what it is.
- [ ] Sort the parts by machine before making any. It shows where the queue will be.
- [ ] Check every flat pattern against the cutter you own: smallest hole, smallest inside corner, material.
- [ ] Write the steps for one part of each kind, then reuse them.
- [ ] Decide what gets checked before a part is called done, and write it on the job sheet.
