The elevator and the wrist share the same space as the funnel, the climber and the camera. Move them in the wrong order and the robot hits itself. One part of the code, the superstructure, owns that problem.

## What we decided

- **One wanted pose at a time.** The driver or an auto asks for a pose, such as L4. The superstructure works out how to get there.
- **The rules are data.** A table says which wrist angles are allowed at each carriage height. A redesigned part changes numbers in one file, not code.
- **Checked every loop.** Fifty times a second, each axis's target is limited to what the other axis's position allows.

## The poses

Carriage height is inches of travel from the bottom. Wrist angle is degrees from level: −90° is stowed pointing down, 0° is level.

| Pose | Carriage | Wrist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stow | 0 in | −90° |
| Station pickup | 1.25 in | −35° |
| L1 | 5 in | −35° |
| L2 | 17.5 in | −35° |
| L3 | 33.3 in | −35° |
| L4 | 65.25 in | −52° |
| Reef algae, low | 8.8 in | +46° |
| Reef algae, high | 24.6 in | +46° |
| Carrying an algae | 1.25 in | +45° |
| Net | 65.25 in | +75° |
| Processor | 1.25 in | +15° |
| Climb | 1.25 in | 0° |

[Setpoints and control](../setpoints-and-control/) draws these twelve as a chart.

L4 and the net stop at 65.25 in, not the full 66 in of travel, to stay 0.75 in short of the hard stops. L1 is not on the CAD's list yet, and the processor pose is marked marginal on the drawings.

## The keep-out table

| Carriage height | Wrist angles allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Below 0.75 in | Stowed only (−90°) |
| 0.75 to 2 in | Any |
| 2 to 7 in | −75° or higher |
| 7 to 9 in | −60° or higher |
| 9 to 15 in | −45° or higher |
| 15 to 23 in | −40° or higher |
| 23 in and up | Any |

The table came from the CAD: 3,256 clash tests of the wrist against every other part, at 15 carriage heights and 12 wrist angles.

### The table as a picture

Each dot is a pose that was tested on the CAD. The shaded area is what the table refuses. Drag the dot, or press a button, and the robot moves to that pose so you can see what the wrist would hit.

- **Some green dots sit inside it.** The table also refuses 17 poses that are clear. That is deliberate: seven simple rows are easier to check and to test than a shape that follows every dot.

### How a move stays legal

- **Stow to L2.** The carriage rises to 1.25 in and stops. The wrist swings out. Then the carriage goes up, with the wrist already at an angle that is legal the whole way.
- **Back to stow.** The same in reverse: down to 1.25 in, wrist to −90°, then the last 1.25 in.
- **Stopping distance counts.** A wrist target must be legal everywhere the carriage could reach before it can stop, not just where it is now.
- **If the wrist angle cannot be trusted,** the carriage freezes where it is.

One more rule sits on top: with an algae in the claw, the wrist stays at +10° or higher and never stows.

## The math: how long does a move take?

The motor controllers do not jump to full speed. They follow a motion profile: speed up at a set rate, cruise, slow down at the same rate.

<Calc id="motion-profile" />

One motor turn moves the carriage 1.375 in: 22 teeth × 0.25 in of chain, × 3 for the three stages, ÷ the 12:1 gearbox.

### Why the planner looks ahead

A carriage at full speed cannot stop where it is. The planner has to judge the wrist against everywhere the carriage could still reach.

<Calc id="stopping-distance" />

At full speed that is about 31 in. The whole band where the funnel limits the wrist is 23 in tall, so a planner that looked only at where the carriage is would be wrong by more than the band.

Holding the elevator still also takes voltage: 0.39 V in the code. [Setpoints and control](../setpoints-and-control/) shows where that number and the other control numbers come from, and lets you change them.

## The climb

The climb is the one move that cannot be undone in a match, so the code is strict about it.

1. **Hold the climb button.** The mechanism moves out of the way, the funnel swings open, and the fork comes down to level. Let go to pause at any point.
2. **Back onto the cage and hold the button again.** The winch winds the fork in and lifts the robot. Pawls stop it letting itself back down.

- On a field, the climb is locked until the last 30 seconds.
- Once the funnel opens, the mechanism stays in the climb pose, even if the code restarts.
- After a missed cage, a second button lets an empty fork back down. It refuses if the robot is hanging.
- After every climb, the pit crew stows the fork, latches the funnel and presses a reset on the dashboard.

## What went wrong

**The first keep-out table gave the wrist no way out of stow.** Read literally, it allowed only stow below 1.25 in and only −75° or higher above it, and the wrist stows at −90°. Looking again at the clash results showed that every angle is clear at exactly 1.25 in. Measuring further on the CAD gave a band from 0.75 to 2 in where the whole swing is clear. The code now stops the carriage at 1.25 in while the wrist leaves or returns to stow.

That band still has to be checked on the real robot: by hand first, with the carriage at 0.75, 1.25 and 2 in.
