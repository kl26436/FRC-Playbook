No robot does everything well. This chapter turns [the read of the game](../read-the-game/) into a ranked list, so the design time goes to the things that win matches.

## What we decided

Each job is a **must**, a **should** or a **could**. The robot is designed around the musts. Shoulds are added if they do not hurt a must. Coulds are only taken if they come free.

| # | Job | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Drive well and never break down | must |
| 2 | Score on L4 from the coral station | must |
| 3 | Climb the deep cage | must |
| 4 | Score a coral in auto, every match | must |
| 5 | Pull algae off the reef | should |
| 6 | Score on L2 and L3 | should |
| 7 | Put algae in the net | could |
| 8 | Pick coral up off the floor | could |

## Why

- **Drive well.** Cycles are short and contact is legal almost everywhere, so a fast, low, tough swerve base is worth more than any one mechanism.
- **L4 from the station.** 12 branches, 5 points each, 7 in auto, and no algae in the way. An alliance should fill L4 first.
- **Deep climb.** 12 points for a few seconds of work. With one parked partner it is the Barge RP.
- **A coral in auto.** Leaving plus one coral is a ranking point. Three L4 coral in auto is 24 points from one robot: 3 for leaving and 7 for each coral.
- **Algae off the reef.** This opens 18 branches. Getting the ball off is the valuable part. The first two go in the processor for Coopertition.
- **L2 and L3.** The same mechanism at lower heights, for the Coral RP and for when L4 is full.
- **Algae in the net.** 4 points with nothing handed to the opponent, but it needs about 100 in of reach and a trip to the barge.
- **Floor pickup.** Useful for dropped pieces. Not needed while the stations feed reliably.

### Processor or net?

A processed ball is 6 points for you and a free throw worth 4 for the other alliance's human player. If their thrower is good, the processor nets you 2. The net is a clean 4.

Process two early for Coopertition. After that, net them if the robot can. If it cannot, process them anyway: the processor is next to your reef, and the ball has to leave the reef either way.

### What to watch for

- One defender may cross to your side. The reef zone and barge zone are safe: contact there is a major foul. The coral stations are not protected, so expect a defender at one. There are two stations, so use the other.
- Some fouls hand the other alliance a ranking point. Knocking a coral off their reef gives them the Coral RP. Touching their cage, or a robot on its cage in the last 20 seconds, gives them the Barge RP.

## The math: where does a cycle's time go?

These distances are from the field drawing, measured robot centre to robot centre.

| Trip | Distance | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coral station to its two nearest reef faces | 11 to 12 ft | About 2 s each way. Pick-up and release time matter as much as top speed |
| Coral station to the far faces | 15 to 18 ft | Fill the near faces first |
| Reef to processor | 8 to 11 ft | A cheap detour from the processor-side faces |
| Reef back faces to the net | 8 ft | Cheap from the back faces, expensive from a station |
| Starting line to the back reef face | 71 in | Any robot can drop a preload in under 2 s |

With the default numbers, driving is about half the cycle. The other half is handling. Taking half a second off the pick-up saves as much time as raising the average speed from 6 to about 7 ft/s.

## A three-robot plan

Two robots cycle L4 from opposite stations. The third strips algae, runs two to the processor, then fills L2 and L3 or defends.

In qualification matches your partners change every match. Be the robot that can do all three jobs, because somebody has to take the algae off.
