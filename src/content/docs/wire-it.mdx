The robot has three kinds of wire.

| Kind | What it looks like | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power | Red and black, thick | Carries the battery's power to every motor and box |
| CAN | Yellow and green, thin, twisted together | Lets the roboRIO talk to every motor and smart sensor |
| Signal | Thin, three wires to a plug | Two sensors and three servos that plug straight into the roboRIO |

:::caution[This is the plan]
Nothing is wired yet. Wire lengths and where each wire is tied down will be settled on the real robot.
:::

The boxes are already mounted by this point. Where they sit, and why, is on the [electronics page](../subsystems/electronics/).

## Power

Power leaves the battery on one thick pair of wires and reaches the power hub. The hub splits it up, and every branch has its own breaker or fuse.

<PowerPath />

- **Keep the thick wires short.** Aim for under 24 in for each one, laid along the cross member. A long wire wastes voltage.
- **The battery's terminals and connector point right**, toward the main breaker on the right rail.
- **Drive motor wires run along the rails.** Leave a loop of spare wire at each module so the module can come off.
- **Lift, wrist and claw wires go up the cable chain** on the right side. Use high-flex cable there, because it bends every time the elevator moves.
- **Label both ends of every wire** with its CAN ID or its hub channel.

## The CAN bus

CAN is one pair of wires that many devices share. Every device has a number, its ID, so the roboRIO can talk to one device at a time. The pair goes into a device and out to the next one, like a string of lights.

This robot has two buses. The swerve drive and gyro are on one, and everything above the frame is on the other. If a wire breaks in the elevator's cable chain, the robot can still drive.

Each diagram below reads top to bottom, in the order the wire visits each stop.

<CanBus />

### The rules

1. **Yellow to yellow, green to green.** Keep the pair twisted.
2. **One line with no branches.** Never join three pairs at one point.
3. **An end at each end.** Each bus starts at a box that ends it, and finishes with a 120 ohm resistor. The power hub is in the middle of its bus, so its own end switch is OFF.
4. **Set every ID before the device goes on the robot.** It is much harder once the device is bolted in.
5. **Name the CANivore `canivore`** in Phoenix Tuner X. The code looks for that name.

### Every ID

The robot code holds the same list in one file, `Ports.java`. A test compares that file with this table, device by device. When the robot starts, it calls every device and shows an alert naming any that do not answer.

## Straight into the roboRIO

| Port | What plugs in |
| --- | --- |
| DIO 0 | The elevator's home switch, a magnetic switch on post E-109 |
| DIO 1 | The wrist's absolute encoder |
| PWM 0 | The funnel latch servo |
| PWM 1 | Both climber pawl servos, on one Y lead |
| USB | The CANivore |
| Ethernet | The radio's port marked RIO. The camera goes in the radio's port marked AUX 1 |

The three servos take their power from the roboRIO's 6 V supply. If that supply sags when a servo stalls, move the servos to a REV Servo Power Module on a spare hub channel.

## Before the first power-up

Put the robot on blocks with the wheels off the ground. Leave the main breaker off until this list is done.

1. **No shorts.** Check the battery leads, the battery connector and the breaker for the right polarity, and that neither wire touches the frame.
2. **Each CAN bus reads about 60 ohm** between yellow and green with the power off. Two 120 ohm ends side by side make 60. A reading near 120 means one end is missing.
3. **Every device has its ID** and current firmware.
4. **Weigh the robot.** The limit is 115 lb without battery and bumpers, and the model is [just over it](../subsystems/from-concept-to-design/).
5. **Measure the height** in the starting pose. The limit is 42 in and the model says 38.9 in.

## First power, one mechanism at a time

Run each mechanism by itself, slowly, before running anything together.

### Drivetrain

- Point all four wheels forward and set the four wheel-angle sensor offsets.
- Check that each module steers and drives the right way.

### Elevator

- Move the stages by hand first. The home switch should read at the bottom.
- The carriage travels 0 to 66 in, 22 in for each stage.
- The two lift motors are mirror images, so one follows the other in reverse. Start with a 60 A limit.

### Wrist

- Let the wrist hang stowed, read the absolute encoder, and set that reading as −90°. Level and forward is 0°.
- **Raise the carriage to 1.25 in before the first wrist move.** With the carriage down, the wrist hits the camera and the lift gearbox.
- Turn it slowly to each end. The stops are at −93° and +77°.

### Funnel

- Latch servo: the horn is 10° up when closed and 32° down when open.
- Tilt the robot 10° and fire the latch. The tray must swing fully open onto its stop.

### Climber

- Pawl servos: the horn at 145° holds the pawls down, and 123° lifts them.
- With the ropes off, check that both ratchets lock and will not pay out.
- The two motors run as leader and follower in opposite directions, in brake mode. Start with a 60 A limit.
- To let the fork down: wind in a little, lift the pawls, and let the springs push the fork out of stow. Its weight takes it to level, and it stops at −2°.
- Wind in to about 88° and stop. The hard stop is at 97°.
